# Deathstalker Cover-Art Visual Review

Status: **developer-provided visual research and direction calibration; not asset,
likeness, costume, composition, or runtime approval**.

## Scope and provenance

The developer supplied ten photographed or scanned *Deathstalker* covers for visual
review on 2026-08-23. They included editions of *Deathstalker*, *Deathstalker War*,
*Deathstalker Rebellion*, *Deathstalker Honor*, *Deathstalker Destiny*, and
*Deathstalker Coda*. The numbered observations below refer only to the order of that
submission.

The copyrighted cover files remain owner-local temporary attachments and are not
copied into this repository. Publisher typography, logos, decorative frames,
illustrations, character likenesses, costumes, weapons, and compositions are not
production assets or image-generation targets. This document records paraphrased
high-level observations only.

When a cover conflicts with explicit developer direction, project contracts, or
source text, the cover does not win. Contradictory faces, hair colors, equipment,
and costumes across editions make the set useful as tonal evidence rather than a
canonical model sheet.

## Recurring visual families

### Covers 1, 3, and 6 — formal blue aristocratic action

- Saturated blue and pale fabric are arranged in large readable blocks around a
  narrow human silhouette.
- High or projected collars, belts, boots, slim swords, and compact pistols combine
  courtly presentation with immediate combat readiness.
- Vertical metal architecture and black armored opposition create a controlled,
  severe Imperial contrast.
- Exact white sleeves, chest graphics, collars, blue body garments, armor shapes,
  stair compositions, and figure likenesses are excluded from reproduction.

### Covers 2, 4, 5, 7, and 8 — gritty sword-and-sidearm pulp

- Swords and compact guns appear together as equal parts of one combat identity.
- Dark leather-like construction, exposed mechanical detail, straps, guards, and
  holsters produce a harsher frontier register than the formal blue covers.
- Acid gold, yellow-green haze, and hot amber backlight separate human figures from
  alien, armored, or mechanically dense threats.
- Back-to-back pairs, crowded rebellion scenes, and lone-hero-versus-giant-threat
  staging communicate operatic pressure and extreme scale.
- Exact exposed-torso outfits, black mechanical armor, organic monsters, poses,
  silhouettes, and cover compositions are excluded from reproduction.

### Covers 9 and 10 — cleaner uniformed action

- Dark fitted clothing, controlled blue accents, compact pistols, and forward motion
  provide a less ornate adventure register.
- A bright environment behind darker figures preserves clear action silhouettes.
- Crowded troops and foreground creatures reinforce danger through scale and number,
  but are too compositionally dense for the canonical battle interface.
- Exact uniforms, alien creatures, armored troops, running poses, and compositions
  are excluded from reproduction.

## Sword-family observations

Image resolution, cropping, and painterly handling limit exact identification. The
labels below describe broad silhouette families, not historical classifications or
canonical weapon models.

| Cover group | Readable sword family | Confidence | Useful high-level quality |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1, 3, and 6 | Slender straight dueling or small-sword family with an open, swept, or basket-like hand guard | medium | Aristocratic precision, narrow negative space, one-handed coexistence with a sidearm |
| 2, 7, and 8 | Long shallow-curved saber or cutlass family with a narrow point and compact guard | medium-high | Mobile field use, broad readable sweep, asymmetry against a pistol silhouette |
| 4 and 5 | Mixed battlefield blades, including apparently broader or shorter single-handed forms | low | Variety within a rebellion force, but insufficient detail for casing or proportion decisions |
| 9 and 10 | No sword construction is reliably visible | high | These covers support pistol/uniform action only and supply no blade evidence |

Across the readable covers, swords are predominantly human-scale, one-handed, and
compatible with a holstered or active compact firearm. The set does not justify
giant two-handed fantasy blades as the principal-party baseline.

Two independently original Owen study families follow from this evidence:

- **Dueling frame:** a slender straight blade, open mechanical guard, protected hand
  channel, and compact vibration housing integrated near the hilt.
- **Field saber:** a shallow-curved single edge, compact asymmetric knuckle guard,
  and low-profile vibration housing that preserves a clean sweeping silhouette.

Hazel's approved functional vibroblade remains distinct: forward-weighted,
straight-backed, mechanically guarded, and visibly industrial. It may inherit the
one-handed scale and clear hand protection but should not become Owen's dueling
blade or saber with different colors.

Do not reproduce any exact blade curve, point, basket, frame guard, grip, pommel,
housing, pose, or hand relationship from a cover. New prop studies must establish
their own proportions, mechanical logic, and safe gameplay silhouette.

## Direction-approved transferable qualities

- Keep **sword plus compact sidearm** as a defining mixed-technology silhouette.
- Use slender dueling blades, shallow-curved field sabers, and functional
  straight-backed vibroblades as broad study families while designing original
  guards, housings, grips, proportions, and motion language.
- Keep principal heroes human-scale even when enemies, machines, architecture, or
  creatures become enormous.
- Use large saturated character color blocks against darker or strongly graded
  environments rather than distributing equal detail everywhere.
- Combine practical boots, belts, guards, holsters, and protective construction
  with one character-specific tailored or repaired identity layer.
- Reserve acid gold, yellow-green, and hot amber atmosphere for selected encounter
  families, danger states, and promotional key-art studies. It is not the universal
  environment grade.
- Use operatic pressure—overwhelming scale, back-to-back readiness, forward action,
  and dense opposition—as emotional guidance. Do not reproduce the vertical cover
  layouts or their exact figure arrangements.

## Owen calibration

The formal blue covers reinforce a narrow, saturated-blue aristocratic-adventure
read with a pronounced collar, pale underlayer, sword, compact sidearm, fitted lower
silhouette, and boots. The later covers reinforce blonde Owen as a valid project
override but do not establish a canonical face or costume.

Owen's preferred cobalt travel coat may inherit the **contrast logic**—saturated
blue outer identity, pale secondary layer, dark head edge, narrow human scale, and
visible sword/sidearm readiness. It must not copy white balloon sleeves, chest
symbols, a blue bodysuit, exact collars, belts, weapons, or any depicted likeness.
His next prop sheet should compare the original dueling-frame and field-saber
families under identical gameplay-scale conditions.

## Hazel calibration

The later covers reinforce red hair, athletic forward motion, compact firearms,
dark practical layers, and close-quarters confidence. They support Hazel's repaired
industrial competence but do not establish a canonical face or costume.

Hazel's preferred teal-and-ivory field kit may inherit the **action logic**—compact
protection, clear holster access, economical movement, and saturated hair against a
dark collar. It must remain more protective and functional than the exposed leather
or bare-midriff treatments shown on several covers and must not copy their armor,
weapons, poses, or likenesses.

## Production use

- Use this review to calibrate silhouette, value hierarchy, mixed technology,
  atmosphere, scale, and broad sword-family contrast during new A/B concept work.
- Do not place a cover image in a runtime atlas, model sheet, prompt attachment,
  texture, UI panel, repository asset folder, or manifest.
- Do not use a cover as the sole reference for character anatomy, face, costume,
  weapon casing, faction armor, creature design, or environment composition.
- Review generated concepts against both the positive transfers and exclusions
  before presenting them to the developer.

Best production venue: **hybrid**. Keep reference filtering, originality review,
silhouette selection, and Godot integration local; use cloud image generation only
for independently original A/B studies expressed through the approved high-level
qualities rather than through attached cover reproduction.
