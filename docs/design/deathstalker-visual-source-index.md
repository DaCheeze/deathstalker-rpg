# Deathstalker Visual Source Index

Status: **source-backed research; not asset approval**
Updated: 2026-08-23

## Purpose and limits

This index translates visual information from lawful public publisher samples,
five developer-provided local searchable texts, and one ten-cover developer review
set into production art notes. It paraphrases copyrighted material and records a
source locator rather than reproducing full passages or illustrations. It is a
visual-development index, not a substitute for the novels and not a claim that the
reviewed material covers every important description in the series.

The descriptions are also not a command to copy published covers, costume designs,
or recognizable franchise identities. The project uses the novels as story and tone
reference while developing its own final visual language.

## Authority and labels

When references disagree, use this order:

1. explicit developer direction;
2. the project's mechanical and presentation contracts;
3. an explicit description in a lawful novel sample;
4. an inference from repeated descriptions;
5. a new visual proposal awaiting approval.

Each entry below is labeled accordingly:

- **Source:** directly described in the linked public sample.
- **Project override:** an intentional developer-approved divergence from source.
- **Translation:** an art-direction conclusion inferred from the description.
- **Gap:** a detail that the samples reviewed so far do not establish reliably.

Spoiler warning: the source notes touch characters, factions, and later-series
locations without summarizing plot.

## Developer-provided cover-art calibration

edition art contradicts itself on faces, hair, costume, armor, and equipment, so it
is treated as lower-authority tonal evidence rather than a canonical model sheet.
The images remain outside the repository. Paraphrased recurring qualities,
character calibration, and explicit exclusions are recorded in
`deathstalker-cover-art-visual-review.md`.

**Translation.** Retain mixed sword-and-compact-sidearm silhouettes, human heroes
against overwhelming scale, strong character color blocking, practical belts and
holsters, slender dueling-blade and shallow-curved field-saber families, and

limited by crop and resolution. Do not reproduce published outfits, likenesses,

## Wardrobe and character findings

### Hazel d'Ark

**Source.** The opening sample describes Hazel as a tall, lithely muscular young
woman with a sharp face, long unruly red hair, green eyes, and a watchful manner.
Her working uniform has heating elements. She carries a sidearm and a backup knife
in her boot, and the uniform becomes blackened and scorched during the opening
disaster. [Deathstalker, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___1.htm)

**Translation.** Hazel's base costume should remain genuinely functional: integrated
temperature control, hard-wearing boots, accessible sidearm and knife locations,
field repairs, and heat damage states all belong. Functional does not require drab.
Peacock teal, turquoise, emerald, sea-green, cream, ivory, antique brass, and small
saffron accents complement her red hair while making her readable against dark
spaces. Large orange, rust, copper, or red fields should stay away from her head.

### Owen Deathstalker

**Source.** The sample describes Owen as tall and rangy, with trained grace, dark
hair and dark eyes. He puts on his best cloak, buckles on a sword, and carries a
disruptor. A displayed ancestor wears thick furs and a steelmesh tunic. Owen's yacht
combines advanced hardware with rich furs, mahogany, cut glass, leather upholstery,
and a gold-plated hull. [Deathstalker, Chapter Two](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___2.htm)

**Project override.** Owen is blonde in this game. The source's dark hair is recorded
to prevent accidental confusion, but it must not replace the developer-approved
blonde anchor.

**Translation.** Owen can carry aristocratic-adventure color and material without
becoming a ceremonial mannequin: cobalt, cerulean, indigo, royal blue, plum, ivory,
warm leather, black-gold, and limited emerald; a tailored travel coat or cloak over
field layers; steelmesh or protective construction; and one restrained luxury
material. A dark or saturated collar edge must keep blonde hair separate from ivory
or gold trim.

### Pirates, dandies, and conspicuous style

**Source.** Pirate captain Terrence Markee is presented as a deliberately gaudy,
dated dandy in flashing silks and clashing colors. [Deathstalker, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___1.htm)

**Translation.** The setting supports loud color as character information. Deliberate
clashes, outdated cuts, and visibly expensive fabric can communicate vanity, origin,
and social aspiration. Saturation should be composed in two or three large fields,
not scattered evenly across every accessory.

### Imperial court and fashionable nobles

**Source.** The Empress's private chambers use silks and flowers in many vivid hues,
while her public presentation combines armor, furs, and the colors and robes of
office. Court fashion is described as preoccupied with extreme colors and trappings.
Finlay Campbell wears the brightest current silks, polished thighboots, a velvet cap,
a cutaway frock coat, fluorescent silver skin, and metallic hair. Other power figures
appear in severe black robes or black-and-silver armor. [Deathstalker, Chapter Three](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___3.htm)

**Developer direction.** Many aristocrats and nobles in the game wear dark clothes.
This remains true, but it is a population tendency rather than a universal uniform.

**Translation.** Divide the court into complementary visual roles:

- dark power figures: black, blue-black, graphite, deep plum, oxblood, velvet depth,
  armor beneath cloth, and controlled antique metal;
- fashion peacocks: saturated silk blocks, fluorescent or metallic body treatments,
  theatrical cuts, deliberate color clashes, and pristine accessories;
- office and military authority: severe armor or robes with limited scarlet, gold,
  fur, or office-color accents.

This contrast is more faithful and more readable than making every noble uniformly
black or every courtier uniformly rainbow-bright.

### Esper rebels and Rim wear

**Source.** Esper rebels appear in battered leathers and chains, with polished repair
or fastening details and bright colors applied to faces and hair. [Deathstalker,
Chapter Three](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___3.htm)

**Translation.** Rebel and Rim design should combine visibly used utility layers with
personal color: painted hair, bright scarf or lining, hand-finished hardware, repairs,
and scavenged luxury. Wear is evidence of history, not a global brown filter.

### Later-series party color and adaptive clothing

**Source.** Later samples continue the same variety: Jesamine uses layered perfumed
silks in aggressively clashing colors; Brett wears a thermal suit with chameleon
technology; and Rose is strongly identified by fitted blood-red leather. [Deathstalker
Return, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671868/9781625671868___2.htm)

**Translation.** Party color is not an exception limited to early court scenes.
Saturated personal garments, adaptive textiles, thermal systems, and strongly
character-specific materials can all coexist in an expeditionary party. Construction
and state changes should still explain function: thermal sealing, camouflage panels,
repair seams, and damaged variants rather than decorative color alone.
## Technology, faction, and environment findings

### Psi-blockers

**Source.** An esp-blocker concealed in the Imperial throne is a translucent cube
about the size of a human head. A living esper brain has been removed and held in
suspension; an applied current keeps it awake and functioning so nearby esper power
is suppressed. [Deathstalker, Chapter Three](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___3.htm)

**Project enhancement.** The game explicitly uses artificial pain-receptor triggering
and the resulting constant psychic agony as the suppression mechanism. It also treats
psi-blockers as destructible speed-zero field objects rather than an encounter flag.

**Translation.** Preserve the source's intimate head-scale translucent containment
inside a larger grounded life-support and field-control housing. The horror should be
clinical and institutional: suspension medium, perfusion, electrodes, restraint,
pain-induction hardware, and visible failure states. Do not turn it into a clean
pylon, mystical shrine, or armed enemy.

### Shub robots and spaces

**Source.** Later public samples describe Shub bodies as roughly humanoid,
highly stylized, almost art-object constructions with polished blank faces and two
luminous eyes. Their artificial world contains steel caverns filled with alien
technological marvels, and their ships can be enormous. The prose's explicit robot
material is blue steel. [Deathstalker: Legacy sample](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671851/9781625671851___3.htm) and [Deathstalker: Coda, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671875/9781625671875___2.htm)

**Developer-provided source.** The extended corpus distinguishes three machine-body
uses. Core robots are repeated manufactured bodies. Ghost Warriors operate a
recognizable corpse through implants, staples, and internal metal, using the dead
identity as psychological warfare. Furies can pass as human before exposing a
machine face, integrated blades, and weapon apertures. Shub itself is an artificial,
partly higher-dimensional metal world with irregular tower/bunker masses, nonhuman
steps and corridors, building-scale moving machinery, cable jungles, variable
gravity/pressure, and temporary human-safe envelopes. See `Deathstalker Summary
2.txt`, lines 4230–4385; `Deathstalker Summary 4.txt`, lines 5507–6309; and
`Deathstalker Summary 5.txt`, lines 11988–12180.

**Project override.** Shub use red, rust, ember, dark crimson, and iron-black rather
than blue or purple.

**Translation.** Retain the source's stylization, blankness, repetition, polished
precision, huge machine scale, and art-object unease while applying the approved
rust-red material family. Oxidation follows seams, heat, service, and exposure rather
than becoming random orange noise. Core machines, Ghost Warriors, and Furies need
separate silhouette/state briefs. Dead flesh on a Ghost Warrior is payload, not a
zombie faction palette. A Fury's human shell and inner chassis must share physical
anchors so the reveal reads as transformation rather than a swapped sprite.

### Haden, Hadenmen, and the Pit

**Source.** The Pit on Haden is a man-made crater with steel corridors constructed to
surround and contain the Madness Maze. A later sample gives Tobias Moon faintly
glowing golden eyes, a disruptor built into his left wrist, internal sensors,
reinforced sockets, accelerated repair, and other embedded systems while retaining a
human-scale body. [Deathstalker: Coda, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671875/9781625671875___2.htm) and [Deathstalker: Destiny sample](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671844/9781625671844___2.htm)

**Developer-provided source.** Haden ships omit ordinary human ergonomics, connect
augmented crew directly to machinery, and use subtly hostile angles, light, and
surfaces. Haden cities combine cavern-scale towers, suspended paths, sharp internally
lit structures, integrated bodies, a low pulse, and spatial behavior suggesting the
city and group mind are one system. New Haden shows this grammar overwriting an
occupied human city. See `Deathstalker Summary 2.txt`, lines 58–258 and 4554–4684;
`Deathstalker Summary 3.txt`, lines 8–78; and `Deathstalker Summary 4.txt`, lines
6414–8480.

**Project override.** Hadenmen are visually golden fortress-machines. The samples
reviewed support golden eyes and embedded augmentation, but do not by themselves
establish a universal full-body gold palette.

**Translation.** Use engineered-gold structural armor over black machinery, with
pale-hot or white cores and human-scale augmentation logic beneath the fortress
massing. Haden environments should combine oppressive steel containment, vast
human-built infrastructure, and the uncanny central exception of the Maze rather
than borrowing Imperial ornament. In an occupied location, preserve legible human
remnants while sharp Haden structures replace, absorb, or plug into them. Ships,
cities, and combatants should feel like scales of one collective machine language.

### Force shields

**Source.** Personal shields are arm-mounted; advancing troopers can carry swords
while the shields audibly operate on their arms. A force screen is close to invisible
when viewed face-on, but its boundary shimmers at the edge of vision and makes the
space beyond appear ghostlike. Large gravity barges visibly shimmer, prow-focused
vehicle shields can ram through heavy steel gates, and ship shields absorb repeated
energy impacts while draining major power. [Deathstalker, Chapter Three](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___3.htm) and [Deathstalker Return, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671868/9781625671868___2.htm)

**Translation.** Base equipment should show the emitter and mounting while the field
itself remains runtime VFX: localized edge distortion, restrained shimmer, audible
instability, and a much stronger contact ripple at the struck arm, weapon path, or
vehicle prow. Avoid a permanently opaque glowing bubble.

### Disruptors

**Source.** A handheld disruptor is compact enough to conceal inside loose clothing
and is normally treated as a hip weapon. Early weapons use an energy crystal and
require roughly two minutes between shots. The concentrated beam can be widened at
maximum dispersal to bore a human-scale passage through steel, leaving the cut metal
extremely hot. Older ship cannon can take about three minutes to recharge, while a
large Imperial array staggers many cannon to sustain pressure. Later technology
reduces personal recharge to roughly thirty seconds. [Deathstalker, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___1.htm), [Chapter Two](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___2.htm), and [Deathstalker Legacy, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671851/9781625671851___3.htm)

**Gap.** The samples establish scale, crystal-based charging, placement, beam effect,
and cooldown, but not a stable casing color, barrel profile, decoration system, or
canonical beam hue.

**Translation.** A compact elite sidearm with a readable crystal or charge-status
component is source-compatible. The project's perpendicular ring capacitor remains
a proposed original silhouette, not a source fact. Keep ready/spent illumination out
of the base color asset and communicate it with light, icon, value, and plain
language at runtime.
### Starships and interiors

**Source.** An Imperial starcruiser dwarfs Hazel's pirate vessel and carries hundreds
of disruptor cannon, shields, and assault torpedoes, though the sample does not lock
a precise exterior silhouette. The pirate ship's bridge is cramped and low-ceilinged,
packed with stations, terminals, sensors, and fire control; broken lights, short crew,
and unreliable systems make labor and repair visible. The Imperial bridge is its
institutional opposite: quiet, efficient, fully staffed, ordered around a command
chair and dominant viewscreen. Owen's yacht has a reinforced gold-plated hull and
mixes rich floor furs, modern computers, leather seats, mahogany, cut glass, carpet,
and a wall-sized viewscreen. [Deathstalker, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___1.htm) and [Chapter Two](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___2.htm)

**Source.** Imperial landing forces use pinnaces, shielded gravity barges, armored
marines, and hulking gleaming-steel war machines covered in weapons. Shub vessels are
described as huge, but the accessible passages do not establish a reliable hull
shape. [Deathstalker Return, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671868/9781625671868___2.htm) and [Deathstalker Coda, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671875/9781625671875___2.htm)

**Developer-provided source.** Haden ships are enormous, sleek, golden, heavily
shielded, and fast, while their interiors subordinate human comfort to direct
machine integration. A separate alien vessel is a vast corpse-white cable cocoon
whose tunnels and egg-like chambers open around occupants; it has no recognizable
human drive, weapon, or bridge arrangement. See `Deathstalker Summary 2.txt`, lines
58–258 and 1619–1863.

**Translation.** Ship and bridge design should reveal class and labor. Noble craft
can combine old-world luxury materials with nearly invisible advanced systems;
working vessels should look crowded, repairable, and crew-intensive; military craft
should communicate overwhelming scale and logistics without inventing a single
universal hull silhouette. The organic cable vessel is its own biomechanical family,
not a Shub variant.

### Golgotha and Imperial Court environments

**Source.** Golgotha's elite surface is described through delicate towers and pastel
cities, while the palace is hidden far underground inside a geothermal steel bunker
roughly a mile and a half wide. Its baseline uses gleaming steel and brass, immense
open chambers, and intricate gold-and-silver pillars. Palace-controlled underground
trains use steel window shutters and visibly exposed lethal-gas outlets as deliberate
intimidation. Lionstone then transforms court sessions through holographic spectacle;
the sampled court is staged as a humid swamp with foul shallow water, mist, twisted
vegetation, insects, and potentially real hazards. Her black-iron and polished-jade
throne rises above that setting, while concealment holograms can hide officials and
killers in plain sight. [Deathstalker, Chapter Three](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671806/9781625671806___3.htm)

**Source.** A later reformed court deliberately changes the language to warm woods,
interlocking arched beams, glowing floor mosaics, black velvet curtains, golden
thrones, and monumental stained-glass heroes. The later skyline combines luminous
towers and delicate bridges with globes, pyramids, minarets, and monoliths.
[Deathstalker Legacy, Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671851/9781625671851___3.htm)

**Developer-provided source.** The extended corpus adds crimson-dawn flight between
pastel towers; contrasting family/corporate tower silhouettes; and another hostile
Court state, an arctic waste with cold, snow, mist, and ice structures over the same
institutional setting. The later warm Court, organic steel-and-glass Parliament,
luminous skyline, hidden Rookery, and still-buried old Court confirm that era and
social function must be labeled. See `Deathstalker Summary 2.txt`, lines 440–520 and
3205–3315; and `Deathstalker Summary 5.txt`, lines 250–330, 913–950, 3249–3290,
3827–3920, and 6104–6145.

**Translation.** Imperial space is not one uniform gothic-industrial kit. The source
supports four distinct branches: pastel elite city, buried coercive machine-palace,
mutable theatrical court, and later warm ceremonial myth-making. Environment A/B
briefs should declare which era and social function they translate rather than
blending all four into generic dark arches.
### Mistworld

**Source.** Later samples summarize Mistworld through severe cold, snow, ice, dense
fog, scarce comforts, bounty hunters, street-fighting experience, and strong
defenses. The official edition listing identifies it as a lawless Rim world protected
by powerful espers and used as a haven by criminals and rebels. [Deathstalker: Coda,
Chapter One](https://www.baen.com/Chapters/9781625671875/9781625671875___2.htm) and
[Mistworld official Google Play/JABberwocky edition](https://play.google.com/store/books/details/Simon_R_Green_Mistworld?id=581mEQAAQBAJ)

**Developer-provided source.** Mistport contains a mostly deserted lamp-marked
starport, one conspicuous steelglass control tower, narrow irregular streets,
ramshackle merchant buildings, and visibly richer guild quarters with pavements,
electric lamps, carved wood, glass, and older gothic arches. Snow, fog, sleet, slush,
rooftops, and quarter-to-quarter class contrast recur. See `Deathstalker Summary
3.txt`, lines 331–415, 951–990, 1239–1265, and 1528–1565.

**Translation.** Mistworld should prioritize cold occlusion and hard-lived settlement:
layered fog, snow and ice accumulation, compressed inhabited silhouettes, warm
interior pinlights, and practical visibility aids. Mistport is not uniformly poor or
uniformly low-tech; district construction and lighting communicate class, guild
power, and access to reliable infrastructure.

### Gehenna

**Developer-provided source.** Gehenna is a black, cracked world engulfed by
continent-scale scarlet and gold fire. An Imperial base stands above ground under a
force screen as a political demonstration; when breached it becomes a fractured dark
fortress with fire visible through cracks, flooded or sprinkler-wet interiors, and
heavy sealed environment armor. See `Deathstalker Summary 2.txt`, lines 1179–1523.

**Translation.** Gehenna needs one overwhelming environmental source—the exterior
fire—held behind black terrain and fortress silhouettes. Wet reflective interiors,
hard-suit staging, and cracks to the fire field provide depth. Fire bloom must not
erase the combat lane, UI, or character edges.

### Technos III

**Developer-provided source.** Technos III combines a fractured metal plain, scrap
hills, trenches, craters, a fortified factory, and a deep honeycomb of inhabited
metal tunnels. Its seasons last only two days and swing through monsoon, lethal sun,
hurricane wind, and blizzard; the factory screen creates a controlled island inside
that weather. See `Deathstalker Summary 2.txt`, lines 6599–6745, 8438–8488, and
8658–8710.

**Translation.** Build one stable factory/tunnel spatial package with weather-state
overlays rather than unrelated seasonal paintings. Surface scrap, trench geometry,
factory mass, and the lower tunnel network should remain recognizable while snow,
rain, wind-carried debris, heat, fire, and damage states change.

### Shannon's World / Summerland

**Developer-provided source.** The pleasure world begins with unnaturally perfect
green and blue pastoral color, designer calm, and conspicuous absence. It progresses
through simplified primary-color welcome architecture and toy settlements, war
damage and fortification, one-dimensional scenic cities, a dark artificial river,
and finally an ancient forest that collapses the bright play-world into perpetual
twilight. See `Deathstalker Summary 3.txt`, lines 5696–6410, 7015–7060,
7528–7555, 8784–8810, and 9148–9180.

**Translation.** Horror here depends on palette and expectation reversal. Preserve
high-key primary fields, simplified shapes, false scale, silence, and punctual damage
instead of applying the global dark grade to every plate. The forest transition is a
deliberate value and material break, not the look of the whole world.

### Virimonde and mechanization

**Developer-provided source.** Virimonde is a broad pastoral food world of fields,
stone boundaries, woods, rivers, old paths, settlements, and an immense stone
Deathstalker Standing. Imperial mechanization proposes and then begins replacing
that human landscape with endless sheds, stacked production, smoke, straight-line
war machines, and erased towns. See `Deathstalker Summary 3.txt`, lines 9533–9770,
9898–9935, and 11182–11212.

**Translation.** Treat mechanization as an invasive before/after layer family. Keep
recognizable horizon, road, wall, or Standing anchors while industrial mass and war
damage consume the pastoral scene. This contrast is stronger than designing
Virimonde as an industrial planet from the outset.

### Later Golden Age Logres

**Developer-provided source.** The later city uses a warm timber Court with glowing
wood mosaics and stained glass, an organic steel-and-glass Parliament, bright towers
and bridges, geometric monuments, and designed civic optimism. Its polished surface
still contains an erased-map undercity and the abandoned old Imperial Court far
below. See `Deathstalker Summary 5.txt`, lines 56–950, 3243–3920, and 6104–6145.

**Translation.** This is an era-specific successor language. It can support later
content, memory, myth, or contrast, but must not be blended into the decaying-Empire
baseline. The vertical coexistence of bright civic surface, hidden undercity, and old
buried power is more useful than a simple “good future equals clean architecture”
reading.

## Production wardrobe grammar

The source-backed wardrobe conclusion is not “make everyone brighter.” It is:

| Population | Base construction | Color behavior | Material cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Principal party | Functional travel and combat layers with individual tailoring | Two or three substantial identity colors plus a neutral | Repaired luxury beside field hardware |
| Dark nobles | Long controlled silhouettes, armor under cloth, precise tailoring | Near-black bases with selective lining or metal contrast | Velvet, dense weave, polished composite |
| Fashion courtiers | Theatrical and current or deliberately dated cuts | Saturated silk blocks, deliberate clashes, metallic treatments | Silk, velvet, polished boot leather, body fashion tech |
| Rebels and Rim crews | Battered utility layers, visible repairs, scavenged protection | Bright personal color in hair, paint, scarf, lining, or one major garment | Worn leather, chains, patched technical cloth |
| Imperial military | Human armor and ordered uniform construction | Dark material base with controlled scarlet, amber, office, or unit accents | Steel, ceramic, dense cloth, restrained brass |

For Hazel and Owen, four new costume directions are being reviewed:

- practical rebel-pulp;
- flamboyant aristocratic adventure;
- exuberant Rim-adventurer patchwork;
- court-rebel fusion.

All faces, costumes, symbols, and final identities remain proposed until the
developer selects a direction.

## Open research gaps

The public samples and developer-provided five-text corpus do not establish every
exterior silhouette, material, or color system. In particular, the following still
need a more focused source pass or a developer-approved original proposal:

- a definitive disruptor exterior design;
- complete architectural vocabularies for every major world;
- consistent full-body construction across all Hadenman eras beyond the project's
  approved golden fortress-machine override;
- a comprehensive character-by-character costume chronology;
- a final Shub core-machine/Fury/Ghost-Warrior silhouette family;
- final Court state selection and the canonical Godot shell shared between states;
- final production priorities among the expanded world families.

Future developer-provided texts should use the same filename, chapter, and search-
phrase locator policy without copying prose into project documents.
